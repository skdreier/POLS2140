## About the course

POLS 2140 teaches students to understand and conduct statistics-based scientific research about politics. By the end of the course, students will:

- Understand the fundamental components of social science research;
- Gain experience interpreting statistical and experimental evidence;
- Be able to implement basic statistical analysis in **R**;
- Think critically about how data, evidence, and empirical analysis can reinforce or challenge the unearned dominance of certain social groups over other minoritized groups.

Along the way, each student applies the scientific method to a political topic that matters to them. The course has no prerequisites — no prior statistics or coding experience is needed.

**Course units**

1. **Course frameworks** — research in the real world; data as subjective
2. **Designing your research** — research ideas, theory, concepts and measurement, variable types and descriptive statistics, research designs
3. **Analyzing data** — probability, bivariate hypothesis testing, bivariate and multiple regression, regression interpretation, experimental design

**Main text:** Kellstedt, Whitten and Tuch. 2023. *The Fundamentals of Social Science Research.* Cambridge University Press. Additional readings (D'Ignazio and Klein, *Data Feminism*; Spiegelhalter, *The Art of Statistics*; Wheelan, *Naked Statistics*) are posted on Canvas.

**Navigating a slide deck**

- **→ / Space** — next slide (or next bullet point, on slides that reveal content step by step)
- **←** — previous slide
- **Esc** or **O** — overview of all slides; click one to jump to it
- **F** — full-screen mode
- **Menu icon (bottom left)** — table of contents
- **To print or save as PDF:** add `?print-pdf` to the end of the deck's URL, then use your browser's Print → Save as PDF.

Slides work on phones and tablets too — swipe left and right to move between slides.
